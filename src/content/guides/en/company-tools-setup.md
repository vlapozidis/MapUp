🧰 Before you can do much of anything else, you need to actually be able to log in everywhere. This is the unglamorous setup that everything else quietly depends on.

## Why this matters now

Almost every task in this role touches at least one of the systems below — a ticket, an announcement, a file on the intranet, a call with someone outside the building. Get these working today, and the rest of onboarding stops being interrupted by "wait, I can't get into that."

## 🧰 What to get set up

- **Email & calendar (Outlook)** — your company account, separate from any personal Microsoft account you might use elsewhere (like for training on Microsoft Learn). Confirm you can send, receive, and see your calendar.
- **Team chat (Microsoft Teams, or your team's chat tool)** — for the day-to-day back-and-forth that doesn't belong in email. Get added to your team's channels.
- **Intranet** — the internal site for company announcements, policies, and reference docs. Worth a bookmark; you'll be back.
- **Single Sign-On (SSO)** — one login that unlocks most of the connected systems you'll use. If SSO isn't working correctly on day one, it tends to quietly block everything else too. Flag it to IT or People immediately if something doesn't work — don't sit on it.
- **Remote access tools (VPN / remote desktop)** — for reaching internal systems from outside the office. They're not the same thing: a **VPN** gets your traffic *into* the network; **remote desktop** gets you *onto* a specific machine already inside it. You may need one, both, or neither depending on your role.

## 🏷️ What you'll be using

`Outlook` `Microsoft Teams` `Intranet` `SSO` `VPN`

## 💡 Tips

- If SSO fails, don't assume it's you doing something wrong — this is one of the most common day-one snags, and IT/People can usually sort it in minutes once they know.
- Bookmark the intranet homepage now. It's easy to forget it exists until the day you actually need it.
- VPN connections can be flaky on hotel or public Wi-Fi — if remote access misbehaves off-site, that's the first thing to suspect.
