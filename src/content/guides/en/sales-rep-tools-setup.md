🧰 None of this is complicated, but it's a lot easier to learn the product when you're not also hunting for where to log a call. Let's get your stack sorted first.

## Why this comes first

A rep who's still figuring out how to log a call in week two is a rep who's losing pipeline data every single day before that. Get comfortable in these tools now, and the product and CRM-fundamentals steps that follow are about learning to sell well — not about hunting for buttons.

## 🧰 Tools to get set up in

- **Your CRM** — the company's CRM is where every call, email, and deal gets logged. Your onboarding contact will get you an account and a quick walkthrough — ask if you haven't been added yet.
- **Dialer software** — the tool that places, logs, and (usually) records your calls, syncing them straight into the CRM so you're not manually typing up notes after every conversation. Same story: your team will set this up and show you the ropes.
- **Email & calendar** — most likely **Outlook**, since that's the company-wide standard elsewhere in onboarding. Make sure notifications are on and you know how to book a meeting — you'll be doing a lot of both.
- **LinkedIn** (and **Sales Navigator**, if your team uses it) — worth setting up your profile now even though prospecting comes later on this roadmap. A complete profile makes a better first impression when a prospect checks you out before a call.

## 🏷️ Skills you'll pick up

`Company Tools` `CRM Basics` `Dialer Software`

## 💡 Tips

- Log into everything on day one, even if you don't touch it yet — a login problem is much easier to fix in your first week than the morning of your first real call.
- Ask your onboarding contact for the specific CRM/dialer accounts you need — company setups vary, and this guide deliberately stays generic.
- Don't over-invest in LinkedIn/Sales Navigator polish right now; a solid profile is enough for today, prospecting technique comes later.
